Prototype / first edition

A piece of furniture that ages with its designer.

Shelving System 01 is built from bent steel sheet that locks into itself. The folds that give each panel its strength are left visible; the joints are where you can read how the piece stands up.

The steel is untreated on purpose. It will oxidise, mark and darken with use. I am keeping this prototype, and I intend to watch it change — alongside the practice it started. It is not presented as a finished product, but as the first documented object in a body of work.

04 Philosophy

Rather than hiding how a piece is made, its construction becomes part of its character.

I design furniture from the construction up.

I am interested in the relationship between material, structure, function and space. Rather than hiding how a piece is made, I believe its construction can become part of its character.

My work starts with making. Ideas are developed through drawing, building, testing and using. A prototype is not simply a step towards the final product; it is part of the design process itself.

I design functional objects with a sculptural quality. Furniture should serve a purpose, but it can also change the way a space feels, invite curiosity and develop a character of its own.

Materials are allowed to show where they come from. Steel can age. Surfaces can change. Colour can bring contrast, warmth and personality.

My ambition is to develop a recognisable design language across furniture, objects and eventually the spaces they inhabit.

Furniture is where I begin.

05 About
Photo to come — Stefan in the workshop

Stefan Flikweert is a furniture designer who builds what he draws.

His work begins at the workbench as much as at the desk. Pieces are figured out by making them — cutting, bending, assembling, then living with the result long enough to understand it.

Shelving System 01 is the first object in that practice. The intention is to develop, piece by piece, a design language coherent enough to carry from furniture into objects, interiors and eventually whole spaces.
